# The aterm creature background

Why every window has its role's creature standing behind the session, and the
four numbers that decide how it looks. The window itself is
[the native agent terminal](aterm.md), and the same art reaches the Dock through
[the macOS app bundles](aterm-bundles.md).

**The role's creature stands in the window background, and the tint is what keeps it there.** kitty alpha-blends a background image over the window background, then draws that background back over the result at `background_tint`, so one number decides how much art survives and 0.80 is Kai's, picked against the real identity card. The art is `aterm/icons/<role>.icns` read for its largest PNG rather than a second committed copy, since a re-drawn creature would otherwise leave the background stale. kitty has no anchor, so placement is baked instead: the 512px art is placed once, unresampled, on an otherwise empty 16:10 plate wide enough that `cscaled` leaves it 60% of the window, top right, clear of the column the card and the session text run down. The plate caches under `~/Library/Caches/aterm/creature`, named for the art and the geometry together, because a plate re-cut to a new share is the same bytes in and only the name can tell them apart. `--no-creature` and `ATERM_NO_CREATURE` leave the background flat, and a role with no committed art, a container that will not read, or a cache path kitty would take as a glob does the same. The background is decoration, so no window fails to open over it.

**The glare comes off the fills, because kitty cannot outline a glyph.** There is no text shadow, and the one contrast helper there is, `text_fg_override_threshold`, reads the cell's background color rather than the composited image, so it is blind to the creature and fires the same over an empty window. That leaves the plate as the only lever. A pixel brighter than `creatureGlareKnee` gives up to `creatureGlareDepth` of its alpha on a smooth curve, so the bright fills that compete with text fade while the dark linework carrying the silhouette stays whole, and only alpha moves rather than any color the roster owns. Measured across all eight at this tint, the faint card rows sit near 3.4:1 against the worst pixel behind them before the rolloff and near 4.1:1 after. The analyst art arrived after that measurement and landed inside it rather than moving it: its worst composited pixel sits fourth of eight, within a spread the whole set fits into.

**The knee sits high on purpose, because a rolloff can only pay for legibility with shading.** Fading the highlights and not the midtones compresses the gradient between them, which is a cost no depth setting removes and only a lower one reduces. A first cut at knee 0.46 and depth 0.58 reached 4.5:1 everywhere and altered 28% of the art, which Kai read as a flat belly and dulled palette wood. At 0.62 and 0.30 it alters 16%, touching the specular pops and leaving the midtones, and Kai chose that against a uniform dim that would alter nothing. The alternative is real and stays on the table: presence is a blend toward the window background, so lowering it preserves every ratio inside the art exactly and spends brightness instead, and 15% reaches 4.0:1 with nothing flattened at all.

**At 60% the creature is as tall as the plate, which is what the top inset is spending itself on.** A square creature at 60% of a 16:10 width is 96% of that height, so the 8% inset cannot fit above it and takes the 4% that is left. `cscaled` covers rather than fits, so a window wider than the plate's 16:10 crops a band off each end: on 16:9 that is 5% a side, the inset absorbs it at the top and the feet lose the rest. The head is what the inset protects, since a clipped head reads as broken where clipped feet read as standing on the edge. A share small enough to leave real vertical slack, or `scaled` in place of `cscaled`, would each end the crop, the second by stretching the art 11% instead.
